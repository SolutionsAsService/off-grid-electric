Volts, amps, ohms and watts

Four quantities explain almost everything on this site. Learn them once, then try each in a calculator.

Before you touch anythingThis guide teaches ideas and does not replace a qualified electrician or your local electrical code. Read the safety limits.

The four quantities

Voltage (volts, V)

The push between two points: the difference in electrical potential. A 12 V battery pushes harder than a 1.5 V cell. No push, no current.

Current (amps, A)

How much charge passes a point each second. A phone charger draws about 1 A at 5 V; a starter motor draws hundreds of amps for a moment.

Resistance (ohms)

How strongly a material opposes current. Thin or long wire has more; thick or short wire has less. Resistance turns electrical energy into heat.

Power (watts, W)

The rate energy is used: volts times amps. A 12 V pump drawing 5 A uses 60 W. Watts are a rate; energy is watts over time.

A water-pipe picture (pressure, flow, a narrow pipe) helps with these ideas. It is an analogy: charge in a wire is not water, and the picture fails for alternating current and for magnetism.

Two laws

Ohm's law: V = I x R. Power: W = V x I. Put them together and a current of I amps through R ohms wastes I x I x R watts as heat. That last form is why thin wire carrying a big current gets warm.

Why a 1,000 W inverter needs thick cable

Low voltage is not low energyA 12 V battery will not shock you through dry skin in normal conditions, but a battery bank can deliver hundreds of amps into a dropped wrench or a misplaced cable. The hazard is heat, molten metal and fire.

Energy: watts over time

An appliance rated 120 W that runs for 5 hours uses 120 x 5 = 600 Wh. Batteries and electricity bills count energy: watt-hours (Wh) and kilowatt-hours (kWh, 1,000 Wh).
