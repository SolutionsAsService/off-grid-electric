Size it

From loads to panels, battery and wire

Sizing is arithmetic. Add up what you use, divide by what the sun gives, and cover the nights. Every step is shown, with the margin printed.

Before you touch anythingThis guide teaches ideas and does not replace a qualified electrician or your local electrical code. Read the safety limits.

The steps

  1. List every load with its watts and hours a day (the Simulator carries your list here).
  2. Convert to watt-hours at the battery, counting the inverter's losses for AC loads.
  3. Divide by peak sun hours and the derate to get panel watts; add a margin.
  4. Cover the nights, and days without sun, with a battery sized to its usable depth.
  5. Choose a system voltage that keeps current, wire and fuses reasonable.
  6. Check cable voltage drop, then have the whole design reviewed against your local code.

Cable and fuse

Every cable loses voltage in proportion to its length and the current. Low-voltage systems feel this most, because the same power is more current. Put the cable's length and current in below.

Fuses protect the wire, not the equipmentA fuse or breaker must open before the wire overheats, and the wire's safe current depends on its insulation, temperature, bundling and your code. A battery bank needs a fuse close to its positive terminal, because a short circuit there has nothing else to limit it.
