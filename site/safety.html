Before you build · Safety

What this guide cannot do

It teaches ideas and does arithmetic with typical numbers. It does not know your batteries, your cable, your climate or your code.

Not a designDo not build a system from a calculator. Have the design and the work checked by a qualified electrician or installer, and follow your local electrical code and any permit and inspection rules.

The hazards, in plain words

Battery banks

A battery can deliver very large currents into a short circuit: enough to melt metal, start a fire or cause burns. Fuse close to the battery, use insulated tools, remove rings and watches, and never lay tools across terminals. Lithium and lead-acid batteries each have charging limits and venting or thermal hazards; follow the maker's instructions.

Solar strings

Panels make voltage whenever there is light, and the voltage rises in the cold. A string can be dangerous to touch and its DC arcs sustain themselves. You need DC-rated disconnects, fuses and connectors, and a controller whose input limit exceeds the cold-weather voltage.

Mains voltage (AC)

Inverter output and grid power can kill. Turn off and lock out before working, prove the circuit dead with a meter you have checked on a known live source, and leave fixed wiring to a licensed electrician.

Meters

Use a meter and leads rated for the voltage and the part of the system you are measuring (see the Multimeter page on CAT ratings), and never measure current across a battery or panel.

What to hand to a professional

  • Anything that connects to the grid or to a building's fixed wiring.
  • Cable and fuse selection for a battery bank (the calculators here show voltage drop only).
  • Grounding and bonding, disconnects, and rapid-shutdown or labelling rules for solar.
  • Any system with a string voltage above your controller's limit on a cold morning, or above what you can measure safely.

What the numbers here are

Appliance watts, sun hours, efficiencies and depth-of-discharge limits are typical planning values printed on the Simulator page. They are not measurements of your equipment. Panel, battery and inverter datasheets and your own meter readings replace them.
