Solar panels, ratings and strings

A panel's label says what it makes in a laboratory. This page shows what that becomes in a day, and the one number that can destroy equipment: cold-weather voltage.

Before you touch anythingThis guide teaches ideas and does not replace a qualified electrician or your local electrical code. Read the safety limits.
Panels are live whenever there is lightYou cannot switch a panel off by switching off the loads. A string of panels can carry a dangerous voltage, and a DC arc does not go out by itself. Wiring, disconnects and fuses for panels must follow your local code.

The label

Panel ratings are measured at standard test conditions (STC): 1,000 watts of sunlight per square metre, a cell temperature of 25 C and a defined light spectrum. A real roof is hotter, dustier and rarely at 1,000 W per square metre, so output is lower than the label.

The five numbers on a panel's datasheet
SymbolNameWhat it is
PmaxMaximum powerWatts at STC. The headline rating.
VocOpen-circuit voltageThe voltage with nothing connected. The highest the panel makes at 25 C, and the one that sets safety limits.
VmpVoltage at maximum powerThe voltage while delivering full power. Lower than Voc.
ImpCurrent at maximum powerAmps at full power. Pmax is about Vmp x Imp.
IscShort-circuit currentThe most the panel can push into a fault. Slightly above Imp.

From label to day: peak sun hours

One peak sun hour is an hour of sunlight at 1,000 W per square metre, the STC level. A place with 4.5 peak sun hours receives, over a whole day, as much energy as 4.5 hours of full-strength sun. Typical figures run from about 3 to 6, set by your latitude, weather and season.

Daily energy = panel watts x peak sun hours x derate. A 400 W panel at 4.5 peak sun hours and a derate of 0.75 gives 400 x 4.5 x 0.75 = 1,350 Wh. The derate stands for heat, dust, wiring and charger losses; 0.75 is a typical planning figure, not a measurement of any panel.

Panels in series and parallel

In series the voltages add and the current stays the same. In parallel the currents add and the voltage stays the same. A charge controller has a maximum input voltage; the string's voltage on the coldest clear morning must stay below it.

Two panels with Voc 44.6 V in series make 89.2 V at 25 C. On a morning at minus 10 C the same string is higher still, because panel voltage rises as it gets colder (about 0.3% per degree C for crystalline silicon). A controller rated at 100 V that looks safe on paper can be beyond its limit.

Charge controllers

A controller sits between the panels and the battery. A PWM controller connects the panel to the battery directly and switches it on and off to regulate charging, so the panel works at the battery's voltage. An MPPT controller converts voltage so the panel can run at its best-power voltage, which can harvest more energy, especially with higher-voltage strings or a low battery. How much more depends on conditions and equipment; check the datasheet.

What this page does not cover

Shading, orientation and tilt, mounting, grounding, rapid-shutdown rules, combiner boxes and DC disconnects. These decide whether an installation is safe and legal; ask an installer who works to your local code.
